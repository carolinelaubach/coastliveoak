### Project Proposal

#### Introduction
This project will visually explore the relationship between the presence of emblematic oak species and various neighborhood characteristics in Los Angeles County. We are especially interested in the coast live oak (*Quercus agrifolia*) and its relationship with variables such as race, income, and housing vintage. 

Research [from the US Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/psw/publications/documents/psw_gtr184/032_Dagit.pdf) shows that coast live oaks are resilient in the face of fire, while [accounts from other wildfires](https://sbbotanicgarden.org/insight/can-oak-trees-provide-a-natural-fuel-break/) suggest that these oak trees are effective fuel breaks during conflagrations. Improving our knowledge about 1) oak trees' location in LA County and 2) their concentration relative to demographic and socioeconomic neighborhood traits could inform further research (and, eventually, policy) about the just distribution of a natural resource that provides critical ecosystem services in a fire-prone region. 

In this project, we ask: What is the relationship between coast live oak presence and neighborhood characteristics in LA County? Sub-questions include: Does a larger presence of coast live oaks correlate with higher income areas? Does it correlate with larger white populations? What is the relationship between oak presence and housing characteristics such as vintage and heating-fuel type, both of which may have implications for vulnerability to wildfires and/or urban structure fires?

For our final project, we narrow our analysis to address the following question: Which parts of Altadena, which was largely destroyed by the Eaton Fire, might be good candidates for coast live oak plantings?

#### Data 

Our oak data comes from [iNaturalist](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations?place_id=962&taxon_id=47850).

This crowd-sourced dataset gives us the most accurate available picture of coast live oak presence in LA County. Its primary limitation is that, because it is crowdsourced from users of the iNaturalist app, it may overemphasize the trees' presence in parts of the county with relatively high concentrations of app users. One possible method of validating the crowdsourced data would be to compare it with a vegetation index such as NDVI, which we may explore in the post-midterm portion of this class.

Our demographic datasets come from the ACS 5-year survey for LA County [from 2022](https://www.socialexplorer.com/tables/ACS2022_5yr), and we use the [Tiger/Line shapefile](https://www.census.gov/cgi-bin/geo/shapefiles/index.php) for census tract divisions. 

#### Analysis and Visualizations

Our analysis of coast live oak presence in LA County will help us describe the relationship between tree presence and neighborhood characteristics, of which we are especially interested in median household income, race, and housing vintage. With this comparison, we can answer basic questions such as: Does wealth in the wildland-urban interface correlate with the presence of this important natural fire break? What about race? 

Second, beyond mere description, our analysis will support wildfire resilience planning recommendations and interventions. By comparing the location of coast live oaks against varying socioeconomic factors found in LA County census data, we can better understand which communities to prioritize when planning interventions.

We plan to visualize both levels of our analysis. One set of maps will show the locations of coast live oaks in LA County, wiht different colors indicating different densities. Another set of maps will narrow in on census tracts that we judge to be good candidates for interventions involving coast live oak plantings, neighborhood hardening, and other wildfire mitigation efforts. 

In this project, we hope first to demonstrate what we suspect to be an uneven distribution of the coast live oak across LA County's WUI, and second to gain insights that may inform planning interventions.
